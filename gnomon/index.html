The AR arc points the wrong way

That is almost always the compass. Gnomon shows a calibration state when the heading is unreliable — wave the phone in a figure of eight, and move away from metal, magnets and car interiors. The arc snaps to true north once the compass settles.

Why do a saved spot's times look wrong?

Times for a saved spot are shown in your device's current time zone, not the spot's own. A San Francisco spot viewed from Europe shows sunrise at something like 15:40 local. The events are correct; only the clock they are printed on is yours. Keep this in mind when planning a trip across time zones.

Does it work offline?

Yes, completely. The solar and lunar calculations run on the device from a built-in astronomical engine — no weather service, no map service, no network at all. That also means no cloud cover: Gnomon tells you where the sun will be, not whether you will see it.

Will the arc hide behind that mountain?

No — the AR arc is drawn over the camera image but is not occluded by real terrain or buildings. The arc shows where the sun will be in the sky; whether a hill blocks it is still your judgement.

Can I check a date months ahead?

Yes. The date scrubber moves to any date, and each saved spot has an 8-week planning calendar of golden hour, blue hour and twilight times.

Do I have to give it my location?

For where you are standing, yes — the sun's position depends on it, and the compass heading places the AR arc. For anywhere else, no: save a spot by coordinates and browse it without location access.

Can I use it without the camera?

Yes. The flat plan dome shows the same sun and moon paths from above, no camera needed. Camera access is only asked for when you open the AR view.
